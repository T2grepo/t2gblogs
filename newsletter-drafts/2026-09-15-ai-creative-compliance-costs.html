Sloppy AI Creative Now Carries Real Risk

Since July 27, any listing image, A+ module, or video with a photorealistic AI-generated person needs a "contains-synthetic-performer" metadata tag before upload, or you're exposed to fines starting at $1,000. At the same time, Amazon's detection systems are catching generative "expansion" artifacts and product-to-frame distortion with reported confidence above 94% — two of the most common ways an over-edited image gets a main image suppressed. The good news: the scope is narrower than it sounds, and AI-assisted production is still far cheaper than an all-studio workflow once you know exactly where the new lines are. One Thing to Try This Week

Shoot every product once, for real — in-house or with a low-cost freelancer — and treat that single photo as the base layer for every AI-assisted variant you generate from it. It keeps your product-to-frame ratio and color accuracy defensible if a listing ever gets flagged, and it's the one production cost worth protecting even as you push everything else to AI.
