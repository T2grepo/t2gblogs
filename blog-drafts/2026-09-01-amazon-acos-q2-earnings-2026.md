# Amazon's Ad Business Just Grew 26% in a Quarter — Here's What That Means for Your ACOS

If your ACOS has been drifting up over the last few months and you can't point to a specific mistake that caused it, Amazon's own Q2 2026 earnings call gives you the explanation, even if it's not a comfortable one. Advertising services revenue hit $19.8 billion for the quarter, up 26% year over year — the fastest-growing major line of Amazon's business after AWS, and it added $4.1 billion in year-over-year revenue on its own. Buried in that number is the more useful figure for sellers: advertising revenue grew roughly 8% *per paid unit* sold compared to a year earlier. That's not more sellers advertising — that's the same volume of sales generating more ad spend per sale, marketplace-wide.

CEO Andy Jassy pointed to Sponsored Products as still the single largest driver of that growth, alongside a big push into live-sports inventory — NFL, NBA, WNBA, and NASCAR ad slots that reportedly sold out completely this quarter, with a 2.3x increase in unduplicated reach for advertisers who bought across multiple sports. None of that inventory is Sponsored Products, but it tells you where Amazon is directing demand and dollars, and it explains part of why the core auction has gotten more expensive: more advertiser budget is flowing into Amazon's ad ecosystem overall, and Sponsored Products absorbs a lot of the spillover competition.

The takeaway isn't "nothing you can do." It's that if your ACOS ticked up 1-2 points this quarter, market-wide CPC inflation is a legitimate part of the explanation — and that means the fix isn't panicking and slashing bids across the board. It's being more surgical about where your spend is actually working.

## 1. Separate market drift from your own inefficiency before you touch bids

Pull your account's CPC trend over the last two quarters and compare the rate of increase to your conversion rate over the same window. If CPC is up but conversion rate held steady or improved, a chunk of your ACOS increase is simply the auction getting more competitive — cutting bids broadly will just cost you rank and impressions without fixing the underlying number. If conversion rate dropped while CPC rose, that's a listing or targeting problem compounding a market problem, and it's the one you can actually control fastest. Don't diagnose these together; they need different fixes.

## 2. Split bids by placement, not just by campaign

Amazon's ad surfaces have multiplied — Top of Search, Rest of Search, Product Pages, and now off-Amazon creator content placements that auto-enrolled active campaigns in early August. Each of these converts at a different rate for your specific catalog, and lumping them under one base bid means you're overpaying on your weakest placement to stay competitive on your strongest one. Pull your placement report by campaign and set placement-specific modifiers deliberately: push harder where ROAS is proven, dial back or exclude placements (including creator content, if it isn't converting for you) where it isn't. This is a 20-minute fix per campaign that routinely recovers a point or more of ACOS without losing sales volume.

## 3. Tighten negative keyword hygiene on a shorter cycle

When CPCs rise market-wide, the cost of a loose match type or an untrimmed search term report rises with it — the same wasted click that cost you $0.60 last year costs closer to $0.75-0.80 now. Move your search term report review from monthly to biweekly for your top-spending campaigns specifically. You're not looking for anything exotic, just the same irrelevant or low-converting terms you'd normally catch eventually — catching them two weeks sooner matters more when the underlying cost per click keeps climbing.

## 4. Shift a portion of budget toward retention, not just acquisition

TACOS, not ACOS, is the number that reflects whether your advertising is actually buying growth versus subsidizing sales you'd have gotten anyway. If a meaningful share of your revenue is repeat buyers, review whether your ad budget still assumes every sale needs an ad-driven click to close. Subscribe & Save enrollment pushes, post-purchase email or SMS where you have permission, and simply making sure your organic listing (title, images, A+ content) converts well enough to hold rank without constant Sponsored Products support all reduce how much ad spend is required per unit sold — which is the exact metric Amazon just told investors is rising.

## 5. Budget-pace around known high-competition windows instead of bidding flat

If Amazon is steering advertiser demand into live-sports and event-driven inventory and that inventory is selling out, expect spillover pressure on Sponsored Products CPCs during those same windows — major sporting events, and the retail moments that cluster around them. Rather than running flat daily budgets and bids year-round, build a simple calendar of your category's high-competition periods and either increase budget deliberately to compete (if the ROI supports it) or pull back bids modestly during those windows if it doesn't. Reacting after the fact to a spike in ACOS costs you more than planning around a predictable one.

## The bottom line

A rising ACOS in 2026 is often less about a mistake and more about a marketplace where advertiser demand — and the money chasing it — is growing faster than most sellers' campaign structures have adapted to. The sellers holding ACOS flat right now aren't spending less; they're spending more precisely, at the placement and keyword level, with less of it wasted on clicks and impressions that were never going to convert.
