# Your ACOS Just Went Up and You Didn't Touch a Bid: The Creator-Content Placement Problem

If your ACOS crept up over the last week and you can't point to a single bid change, campaign launch, or seasonal dip to explain it, check your placement report before you touch anything else. On August 10, 2026, Amazon began serving Sponsored Products ads inside Amazon Influencer Program content — product reviews, buying guides, and editorial posts written by creators with verified sales history. No opt-in screen, no pause-and-review step. Any campaign already set to "Increase reach" for off-Amazon delivery was enrolled automatically, at whatever bid it was already running. Campaigns set to "Limit off-Amazon spend," and sellers in markets where creator inventory isn't live yet (the UK, for now), were left alone.

That sounds like a minor delivery tweak. It isn't, and the mechanism is worth understanding because it's a textbook example of how ACOS creeps up without anyone making a bad decision.

## Why This Specific Change Moves Your Numbers

Most active Sponsored Products accounts carry placement modifiers — a heavy bid adjustment on Top of Search, a lighter one on Product Pages, maybe nothing on Rest of Search. Those modifiers exist because sellers learned, through trial and error, which placements convert well enough to justify a premium bid.

Creator content is a new placement, and it doesn't inherit any of that. Your Top-of-Search adjustment does not carry into creator placements. So a campaign that was deliberately bidding up for high-intent search real estate is now also bidding at that same base rate for a completely different kind of impression — one served inside a review or buying guide, to a shopper in a different mindset, with no history to tell you whether it converts at all for your product.

You didn't decide to pay for this inventory. The delivery setting decided for you, and the bid that was calibrated for search terms is now being spent somewhere it was never tested.

## Five Concrete Moves to Make This Week

**1. Find your current delivery setting and make it a decision, not a default.** Open each active Sponsored Products campaign and check whether it's set to "Increase reach" or "Limit off-Amazon spend." If you don't know, it's almost certainly still on the default that enrolled you. This isn't an argument for switching everyone to "Limit off-Amazon spend" — creator placements might work well for some catalogs, especially ones with strong review content. The point is that right now the choice was made for you at scale, across every eligible campaign, without any product-level judgment applied.

**2. Pull the placement report and isolate creator-content spend.** Amazon's placement breakdown now includes this channel separately. Look at spend, clicks, and conversions for creator placements alone over the last week, isolated from Top of Search and Rest of Search. If it's burning budget with an ACOS meaningfully worse than your search placements, that's your leak, quantified.

**3. Set a placement-specific bid adjustment once you have five to seven days of data.** Don't kill the placement blind — creator content can genuinely work, particularly for products with a strong story or a category where buying guides drive real purchase intent. But treat it like any new placement: bid it down until it proves itself, rather than letting it run at your search-calibrated rate by accident.

**4. Re-audit your search term report on the 8-day (Sponsored Products) and 15-day (Sponsored Brands) cadence you should already be using, and don't skip it because "nothing changed."** This particular update is a reminder that ACOS drift often isn't about keywords going stale — it's about delivery mechanics shifting underneath a campaign you haven't touched. Pair your regular negative-keyword cleanup with a placement-level review, not just a search-term one.

**5. Track TACOS weekly, not ACOS campaign-by-campaign.** ACOS on an individual campaign can look fine even while your blended advertising cost against total revenue climbs, especially when a new placement type is siphoning spend across your whole account at once. TACOS is the number that catches an account-wide shift like this one before it shows up as a quarter of disappointing margin. If you're only checking ACOS inside each campaign's dashboard, an issue that spans every "Increase reach" campaign in your account can hide in plain sight.

## The Broader Pattern Worth Remembering

This isn't really a story about one August update. It's a reminder of how ACOS inflation actually happens for most sellers: not through one obviously bad decision, but through defaults, new placements, and settings inherited from months ago that nobody revisits until the number looks wrong. CPCs have already climbed roughly 35% since 2023, and every new ad surface Amazon rolls out — creator content today, something else next quarter — adds one more place your existing bid strategy gets applied without being re-tested. The sellers who keep ACOS under control aren't the ones who found one clever trick; they're the ones who audit placement-level delivery on a schedule, treat every new inventory type as unproven until the data says otherwise, and watch TACOS as the number that can't hide behind a single campaign's clean-looking dashboard.

Start with your placement report this week. If creator content is already a meaningful share of spend with nothing to show for it, that alone may explain the gap between last month's ACOS and this month's.
