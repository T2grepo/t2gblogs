# Your ACOS Isn't Lying to You — It's Just Not Telling You Everything

If your ACOS looks "fine" this quarter but your bank balance doesn't agree, you're not imagining it. Two separate cost increases have hit sellers at the same time in 2026, and neither one shows up cleanly in the metric most sellers use to judge whether their advertising is working.

## What's actually changed

Two things happened this year that most sellers are still underpricing for.

First, cost-per-click on Sponsored Products has climbed hard. Industry benchmarking in 2026 puts average Sponsored Products CPC in the $1.18–$1.34 range, up from roughly $0.99–$1.05 two years ago — a climb of close to a third. Nearly eight in ten brands tracked in recent advertiser surveys report CPC increases, and almost half describe the increase as double-digit. The practical effect: if you spent $500,000 on ads last year, you need something like $550,000 this year just to hold the same impression volume — before you've generated a single extra dollar of revenue.

Second, and this is the part most ACOS dashboards don't capture at all: Amazon's FBA fee restructuring that took effect January 15, 2026 added an average of $0.08 per unit sold, with a range of $0.00 to $0.51 depending on size tier and price band. Small standard items priced over $50 took the worst of it — a 15.4% fee increase on that tier alone. Layer in rising aged-inventory surcharges, inbound defect fees, and low-inventory penalties, and you can lose 15 or more points of margin through line items that never touch your advertising report.

That's the trap. ACOS measures ad spend against ad-attributed sales — it was never designed to catch a fulfillment fee hike. A seller can hold ACOS flat all year and still watch net margin erode, because the damage is happening one layer down, in COGS and fulfillment, not in the campaign manager.

## Why "ACOS looks okay" is the wrong signal to relax on

TACOS (total advertising cost of sale — ad spend as a percentage of *total* revenue, not just ad-attributed revenue) is the better gut-check, because it won't let rising organic sales mask a growing ad dependency. But even TACOS doesn't solve the fee problem. You need a third number sitting next to both: contribution margin per unit, calculated *after* the current fee schedule, not last year's.

Here's the concrete move: pull your per-SKU P&L and rebuild it with January 2026 fee tables, not whatever spreadsheet you built it from in 2024 or 2025. For most sellers this is a half-day exercise and it routinely surfaces two or three SKUs that are now unprofitable to advertise at all, even at a "good" ACOS, because the fulfillment math changed underneath them.

## Five things to actually do about it

1. **Recalculate break-even ACOS per SKU against current fees, not last year's.** Break-even ACOS = (price − COGS − current fulfillment fees − other fees) / price. Any SKU where your current ACOS is close to or above that number is now losing you money on paper even if the campaign report says "efficient."

2. **Shift budget away from size-tier losers before you touch bids.** If a SKU sits in the small-standard, $50+ price band, it absorbed the steepest fee increase (up to 15.4%). Check whether a bid cut can offset that before assuming the fix is "spend less" — sometimes the real fix is a price increase or a size-tier repack, and no amount of bid management solves a structural fee problem.

3. **Move judged-safe spend from broad match to exact and product targeting.** With CPCs up roughly a third industry-wide, broad match is the format absorbing the most waste — you're paying inflated clicks for traffic you were never trying to buy. Tightening match type doesn't fix rising CPCs, but it stops you paying the new, higher price for low-intent traffic you didn't want even at the old price.

4. **Set a TACOS ceiling, not just an ACOS target, and review it monthly against the fee schedule.** A TACOS ceiling catches the scenario where ACOS looks stable but you're leaning on ads more each month to hit the same revenue — a sign organic rank is slipping and you're papering over it with spend.

5. **Audit dayparting and placement reports before your next budget cycle.** With clicks costing more, the ROI gap between your best-performing hours/placements and your worst has widened — the same bid now buys meaningfully different outcomes depending on when and where it serves. A placement and hour-of-day review that took a backseat when CPCs were cheap is now worth the hour it takes.

## The bigger picture

Rising CPCs and the FBA fee restructuring are two independent cost increases that happened to land in the same year, and most seller tooling still reports on them separately — ad platforms show you CPC and ACOS, Seller Central shows you fee changes buried in reports few people open. The sellers getting hurt right now aren't the ones with bad campaigns; they're the ones measuring the old way in a year where the inputs moved.

If you haven't rebuilt your break-even math since January, that's the first thing to fix — everything else follows from having the right number to optimize against.
