# Three Amazon Policy Changes Landing This Month — And What Sellers Should Do About Each One

Most weeks bring one Amazon update worth reacting to. This month brought three, all landing within days of each other and all touching a different part of a seller's operation: catalog, advertising, and creative. If you haven't checked Seller Central in the last two weeks, here's what you missed and what to do before it costs you rank, cash flow, or compliance standing.

## 1. Product Titles Are Now Capped at 75 Characters

Amazon's new title policy took effect August 3, 2026 (after an initial July 27 start date was pushed back), and it's the biggest catalog change in years. Titles across nearly every category — everything except media like books and music — are now limited to 75 characters, down from the roughly 200 characters sellers were used to stuffing with keywords. Amazon has already auto-rewritten over 984 million titles using its own AI recommendations since the policy was announced in June.

To offset the lost space, Amazon introduced **Item Highlights**, a new 125-character field that sits next to the title in search results and on the detail page, meant for materials, use cases, and comparison points. Critically, this field is searchable — it's not just cosmetic.

**What to do:**
- **Don't let Amazon's AI rewrite keep your title.** If your titles were flagged, you get a 14-day window in the Review Listing Changes tool to approve, edit, or reject the AI's version. Check every flagged ASIN — automated rewrites routinely drop the primary keyword, brand name, or a size/color variant that was driving search matches.
- **Rebuild titles keyword-first.** With 75 characters, there's no room for filler. Lead with your primary keyword phrase, then brand, then the single most differentiating attribute. Everything else — materials, compatibility, use cases — moves to Item Highlights, not because it's less important, but because it's still indexed there.
- **Treat Item Highlights as a second SEO surface, not an afterthought.** Sellers who leave it blank or copy-paste boilerplate are leaving searchable real estate on the table that competitors are already using for long-tail keyword coverage.

## 2. Sponsored Ads Billing Now Defaults to Automatic Deduction

As of August 1, 2026, Amazon shifted the default payment method for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display: ad spend is now deducted automatically from your seller account balance (i.e., your sales proceeds) before disbursement, instead of being billed to a credit card. This was originally slated for April 15 but was delayed after seller backlash, including an organized one-day ad boycott from the Million Dollar Sellers community — a sign of how disruptive this is for sellers running tight cash flow cycles.

The practical effect: your ad spend now comes directly out of what you'd otherwise be paid, in near real time, rather than showing up as a separate credit card statement at the end of a billing cycle.

**What to do:**
- **Check your billing settings now.** If proceeds-deduction cash flow timing doesn't work for your business, go to Ads Console → Billing → Payment Method and switch to **Pay by Invoice**, which reverts to a monthly invoice due 30 days later — closer to the old credit card cadence.
- **Re-forecast disbursements for Q3.** If you're on the new default, your payout amounts will be lower and more variable week to week as ad spend fluctuates. Build that into cash flow planning before it surprises your finance team mid-quarter.
- **Reconcile TACOS against net proceeds, not gross revenue**, going forward — the gap between the two just got more visible and more immediate.

## 3. AI-Generated Imagery Now Requires a Disclosure Tag

Since July 27, 2026, Amazon requires sellers to add a specific metadata tag — `contains-synthetic-performer` — to any product image or A+ Content asset featuring a photorealistic AI-generated person. This applies globally, not just in the US marketplace, and covers lifestyle shots, model images, and any A+ module using synthetic humans.

This is Amazon getting ahead of a problem it clearly expects to grow: as more sellers lean on AI image generation to cut creative production costs, Amazon wants a clean way to flag content that isn't a real photograph of a real person.

**What to do:**
- **Audit your current creative library**, especially anything produced in the last year with AI generation tools, for photorealistic human models. Untagged images that should carry the tag put you at risk of listing suppression.
- **Build the tag into your creative workflow going forward** rather than retrofitting it — whoever handles your image production (in-house or agency) should be adding this metadata step by default, the same way alt text became standard practice.

## One More to Watch: Q4 Peak Fulfillment Surcharge

Not a change to act on yet, but one to budget for: Amazon has set its 2026 peak-season FBA surcharge at an average of +$0.32 per unit for anything shipped out of a fulfillment center between October 15, 2026 and January 14, 2027, stacking on top of the 3.5% fuel and logistics fee already in place. If you haven't updated Q4 landed-cost projections and pricing floors to reflect this, now — six weeks out — is the time.

## The Common Thread

Three separate teams at Amazon shipped three separate changes, but they add up to the same lesson: catalog, ads, and creative are getting less forgiving of default settings and unreviewed automation. Sellers who audit their titles, billing method, and creative tagging this week will avoid the scramble competitors will be having in September.
