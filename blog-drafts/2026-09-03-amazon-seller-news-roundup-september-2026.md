# Amazon Quietly Closed the Door on Account Sales and Revenue-Based Loans — Here's What Else Just Changed

Three developments in the last few weeks touch three very different parts of an Amazon business — financing, ranking, and advertising — and none of them got the attention they deserved. If you missed them, here's what happened and what to actually do about each one.

## 1. The August 24 BSA Amendment Reaches Your Lender, Not Just Your Account

Amazon's Business Solutions Agreement — the contract every seller operates under — picked up two new restrictions that took effect August 24, 2026. Amazon had flagged the change back in May, but the practical impact only landed once the effective date hit.

The first restriction bans transferring your account, or your rights and obligations under the agreement, to anyone else without going through Amazon's formal transfer process. The second, broader change explicitly prohibits **pledging** those same rights — meaning your future Amazon payouts — as collateral for a loan. That second clause is the one worth reading twice: it's written to reach revenue-based lending arrangements and account-sale/aggregator deals where a lender's security interest attaches to your payout before it lands in your bank account, not just outright account sales.

**What to do:**
- **Pull any revenue-based financing agreement you currently hold** and check whether the lender's security interest attaches to your Amazon payout directly. If it does, talk to counsel about whether the arrangement now conflicts with the BSA — getting flagged for a contract violation is a worse outcome than renegotiating the loan structure now.
- **If you're mid-negotiation on an account sale or an aggregator deal**, route it through Amazon's account transfer process explicitly rather than assuming a side agreement will hold up. Enforcement risk on undisclosed transfers just went up.
- **Loop in whoever handles your business financing** — this is a legal/finance change, not a marketing one, and it's easy for it to slip past the person managing ads and listings.

## 2. The Featured Offer Eligibility Gate Is Gone — Ranking Is the Only Filter Now

Amazon finished rolling back the separate seller-eligibility gate that used to sit in front of Featured Offer (Buy Box) consideration, with the change live across US, EU, and UK stores and rolling out globally through the rest of 2026. Previously, metrics like chargeback rate, order defect rate, and Voice of the Customer complaints worked as a hard pass/fail checkpoint — fail it, and your offer wasn't even considered for the Buy Box regardless of price or delivery speed.

That gate is gone. Those same metrics haven't disappeared — they've been folded directly into the ranking formula alongside price, delivery speed, and service quality. Every offer now gets considered; what determines whether you win is a single blended score instead of a pass/fail step followed by a separate ranking pass.

This matters more than it sounds like. Sellers who were previously excluded outright because of a rough ODR or VOC patch are now back in the running — which means your competitors who were gated out are suddenly back in your Buy Box rotation, and the metrics that used to only matter for compliance now move the needle on every single impression.

**What to do:**
- **Stop treating ODR, chargeback rate, and VOC as pass/fail compliance checkboxes.** They're now continuous scoring inputs — the difference between a 0.4% and a 0.9% defect rate can move your ranking even if both would have cleared the old gate.
- **Re-check your competitive set.** Sellers who couldn't touch the Buy Box under the old gate may now be live competitors on ASINs where you'd stopped watching pricing closely. Pull a fresh competitor scan on your top revenue ASINs.
- **Weight delivery speed and service quality in your Prime/FBA decisions accordingly** — with the binary gate gone, these factors have more room to differentiate you from a rival with similar pricing.

## 3. Amazon Is Turning Alexa Into a Storefront, and unBoxed 2026 Is Coming Fast

Amazon's Alexa+ Agentic Ads format — first shown at Cannes Lions and now live in beta on Echo Show devices — lets a shopper go from hearing an ad to completing the purchase entirely inside the conversation, no screen tap required. Early partners include Papa Johns for food orders and Ticketmaster for concert tickets. It's early and narrow, but it's Amazon's clearest signal yet that agentic, conversational commerce is where ad formats are heading next, not a side experiment.

That direction will get a lot more concrete at **unBoxed 2026**, Amazon Ads' annual advertiser event, running September 28–30 in San Francisco. Recent regional versions of the event (including unBoxed Toronto) have already previewed AI creative tools and expanded Prime Video ad inventory — expect the September event to bring US-market detail on both fronts, plus likely expansion news for agentic formats.

**What to do:**
- **Don't chase Alexa+ Agentic Ads yet if you're not already a large brand with Amazon's ad team relationship** — it's beta, narrow, and not self-serve. But start tracking it, because agentic checkout inside ad units is going to reshape what "creative" needs to do over the next 12–18 months.
- **Block time the first week of October to review unBoxed announcements** and assess which new formats or AI creative tools are worth a test budget for Q4 or Q1. Being three weeks late to a new placement type is usually the difference between cheap early inventory and a saturated auction.
- **Revisit your creative production pipeline now**, independent of what's announced — if Amazon is investing this heavily in AI-assisted ad experiences, the sellers with clean, well-tagged, high-quality creative assets already in place will be first in line to test new formats when they open up.

None of these three changes is a minor Seller Central notification you can skim past. One rewrites what your financing options look like, one changes how every offer on your catalog gets ranked, and one previews where ad spend is headed next. Sellers who treat all three as background noise will be reacting in Q4; sellers who act on them now will be positioned ahead of it.
