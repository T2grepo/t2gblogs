**Meta description:** Amazon's Price History tool now spans a full year, the Q4 peak fulfillment surcharge window opens October 15, and Accelerate and unBoxed are back-to-back this month. Here's what each change means for your pricing, margins, and Q4 plan.

Amazon rarely announces one thing at a time, and this month is no exception. In the space of a few weeks, Amazon has widened how far back shoppers can see your pricing, confirmed the cost structure for the October–January peak, and lined up its two biggest seller- and advertiser-facing events of the year back to back. None of these are hypothetical — they're either already live or landing within days. Here's what each one actually changes, and what to do about it before Q4 spend ramps up.

## Price History Now Shows a Full Year, Not a Quarter

Amazon has expanded its Price History feature from a 90-day window to a full 365 days, rolled out to shoppers in the US, UK, and India. It's not gated behind Prime or any paywall — anyone can pull it up from the price history link next to the buy box, through the app's price-tracking icon, or by asking Alexa for Shopping (the renamed Rufus) directly. The display shows the lowest Featured Offer price for each day or week in the window, along with the date of the last change.

The practical effect: a "40% off" badge during a Q4 lightning deal now sits next to twelve months of pricing, not three. If your list price only ever moves up seven days before a promotion so the discount looks bigger, that pattern is now visible to any shopper who clicks one link — and increasingly, it's Alexa for Shopping surfacing that history unprompted when someone asks "is this a good deal."

**What to do about it:** Audit your pricing calendar for the last year, not just the last quarter. If you've been using pre-event markups to manufacture bigger discount percentages, stop — that tactic is now trivially visible and works against you with price-conscious shoppers using the tool to sanity-check deals. Build your Q4 promotions around a genuinely stable reference price instead, and treat the yearlong history as a reason to keep pricing consistent rather than choppy.

## The Q4 Fulfillment Surcharge Window Opens October 15

Amazon's 2026 holiday peak fulfillment surcharge takes effect October 15 and runs through January 14, 2027. It's not new information — Amazon confirmed it in August — but September is when the gap between "I saw that announcement" and "I priced it into my Q4 numbers" actually closes or doesn't. Sellers finalizing October inventory placement, promotional budgets, and Q4 margin targets right now are the ones who won't get an unpleasant surprise on their first November settlement.

**What to do about it:** Pull your average per-unit fulfillment cost for the surcharge window and rebuild your Q4 contribution margin with it included, SKU by SKU — don't apply a blanket estimate across your whole catalog, since the surcharge hits differently depending on size tier. If any SKUs go from profitable to marginal once the surcharge is factored in, decide now whether you're adjusting price, pausing ad spend on them for the window, or accepting thinner margins deliberately — not reactively in December.

## Seller Fulfilled Prime's Bar Is Already Higher Than You Might Think

This one isn't brand-new but it's still catching sellers off guard heading into Q4: since July, standard-size SFP offers need a two-day delivery date showing on 75% of Prime page views (up from 70%), a one-day date on 40%, and a five-day date on 90%. Oversize and extra-large tiers have their own, separate thresholds. Amazon measures this by page-view display, not by orders actually delivered on time — meaning a carrier slipping during your highest-volume weeks can pull your qualifying percentage down even if most individual orders still arrive fine.

**What to do about it:** Check your current qualifying percentage in Seller Central now, before Q4 volume makes it harder to recover. If any SFP SKUs are sitting close to the 75% two-day line, either tighten your carrier commitments for those specific listings or move them to FBA for the peak window — losing the Prime badge mid-Q4 because you dropped below threshold is a worse outcome than proactively switching fulfillment for a handful of SKUs.

## Accelerate and unBoxed, Back to Back

Amazon Accelerate runs September 22–24 in Seattle, and Amazon Ads' unBoxed follows September 28–30 in San Francisco. Coming one week apart, they're effectively Amazon telegraphing its seller and advertiser roadmap for the next 12 months in one sitting — new fee structures, fulfillment tools, and ad formats typically get their first public airing at one of these two events, months before formal rollout.

**What to do about it:** You don't need to fly to either city. Both events publish keynote recordings and session summaries within days, and Amazon Ads' newsroom posts unBoxed product announcements as they happen. Block an hour after each event to skim what's new — the sellers who move first on a new ad format or fee change are usually the ones who caught it at Accelerate or unBoxed, not the ones who read about it in a policy email three months later.

## One More Thing Worth Checking

Since August 1, a group of advertisers lost the ability to run ad spend on a credit card as the default payment method — costs now draw from account balance or Pay by Invoice instead. If you haven't confirmed which payment method your ad account is actually using, do it before Q4 spend ramps up; a declined or misrouted payment method in November is the kind of thing that pauses campaigns at the worst possible moment.
