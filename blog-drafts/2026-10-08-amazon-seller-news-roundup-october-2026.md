# Four Things That Just Changed on Amazon — And the One Deadline This Week

Amazon doesn't pause its roadmap for Q4 planning season, and this stretch of October is proof. In the last few weeks, Amazon has quietly merged DSP accounts across three dozen countries, opened Seller Central to orders from four other marketplaces, and put a hard date on a fee-cap program a lot of sellers assumed would just keep running. None of these made front-page news, but each one touches either your ad account, your catalog operations, or your margin math directly. Here's what actually changed and what to do about each one before Q4 spend locks in.

## Your DSP Account Is Being Merged Whether You Asked or Not

Since late July, Amazon has been automatically migrating existing Amazon DSP accounts into a single, unified advertiser account that spans 34 countries — from the US and Canada through most of Europe, parts of the Middle East, and Australia, India, Japan, China, Singapore, and New Zealand. If you run programmatic campaigns, this is not optional and not something you requested: Amazon is rolling it out in waves with no action required on your end, no re-registration, and your old account identifiers (CFIDs) left intact underneath a new advertiser account ID.

The practical change is that Sponsored Ads, DSP, and Amazon Marketing Cloud now live under one login, and multi-country buying — activating programmatic campaigns across the Americas, EMEA, and APAC — no longer requires separate accounts per region. The catch: the merged account shows up under a new "advertiser account" category in the account selector rather than inside Campaign Manager's Advertisers page — exactly the kind of navigation change that causes teams to lose track of reporting mid-quarter.

**What to do about it:** If you or your agency runs DSP in more than one country, check your account selector this week rather than waiting to notice the change mid-campaign. Confirm your AMC audiences and attribution reporting are still pulling from the right account ID, and flag the new navigation to your team now — a missed campaign pause because someone was looking at the old Advertisers page is an avoidable mistake.

## Seller Central Just Became a Multichannel Hub — Use It or Don't, But Decide

As of late September, Amazon opened Seller Central to pull in orders from eBay, Shopify, TikTok Shop, and Walmart Marketplace directly, at no additional cost, on a gradual US rollout. You can connect those channel accounts, import listings, and see and fulfill orders from all of them in one dashboard. Amazon says a combined profit-and-ad-spend view across channels and one-edit listing updates are coming later, but the order consolidation piece is live now.

This is a genuine operational shift, not a gimmick: for sellers already running the same catalog across Amazon, Shopify, and Walmart, it removes one of the standing reasons to keep a separate multichannel order tool in the stack. But "available" isn't the same as "right for you" — importing listings from four other channels into your Amazon dashboard also gives Amazon visibility into pricing and inventory patterns across your whole business, not just your Amazon presence.

**What to do about it:** If order management across channels is genuinely a pain point for you, pilot the connection on one secondary channel first — Shopify or Walmart, not your highest-volume one — before migrating everything over. If you're already paying for a dedicated multichannel tool, don't cancel it yet; wait until the promised unified profit-and-ad-spend view actually ships before deciding whether Amazon's version replaces it.

## The New Selection Program Deadline Is October 31 — Not a Typo

This is the one with a real clock on it. Amazon's New Selection Program, which caps referral fees on new branded FBA ASINs (roughly 10% or your category rate, whichever is lower, on the first 100 units, dropping further on the next 100) and covers storage for qualifying units, has been running since July 30 as an introductory offer. Sellers who enrolled in an earlier version of the program don't need to do anything for ASINs already launched. But to keep getting those fee caps on new branded ASINs you list after October 31, you need to confirm enrollment under the updated program terms before that date.

Miss the deadline and you don't lose benefits already earned — but any new branded ASIN you launch in November or later won't get the reduced referral fee or storage benefit unless you've confirmed enrollment. For anyone planning Q4 or early-2026 product launches, that's the difference between a meaningfully lower landed cost on your first units and paying full freight from day one.

**What to do about it:** Check your enrollment status in Seller Central this week, not after the 31st. If you have new branded ASINs planned for Q4 or Q1, confirming enrollment now costs you nothing and protects the fee cap on launches you haven't even listed yet. If you're not sure whether you're on an older or newer version of the program, treat that ambiguity as a reason to check sooner, not later.

## Ad Payment Auto-Deduction Is Now the Default — Check Which Side You're On

Earlier this year, Amazon's move to deduct ad spend automatically from seller account balance — instead of charging a credit card — triggered a real seller revolt, including a coordinated 24-hour ad boycott organized by Million Dollar Sellers. Amazon delayed the rollout once, to August 1, and offered a one-time $2,500 promotional ad credit to affected advertisers. That date has now passed, and sellers who didn't actively choose Pay by Invoice were automatically defaulted into account-balance deduction.

Combined with the separate change to payout timing — proceeds now release seven days after delivery rather than seven days after shipment — this is a cash-flow double-hit that's easy to miss until a Q4 ad spend ramp collides with slower account-balance replenishment.

**What to do about it:** Confirm in Seller Central which payment method your ad account is actually using right now, not what you remember selecting months ago. If you're on account-balance deduction and plan to increase ad spend for Q4, model your cash flow with the seven-day-post-delivery payout lag included — a campaign that scales faster than your balance replenishes will get paused automatically at the worst possible moment.
