# Amazon Just Rebuilt Its Entire Ad Platform Around AI — Here's How to Actually Win With the New Campaign Types

Amazon didn't tweak its ad platform this week. At its unBoxed 2026 conference on September 29, it replaced the Ads Console and the standalone Amazon DSP interface with something new called **Amazon Ads Agent** — a conversational, AI-run advertising system built around just three campaign types: **Sponsored Ads**, **Display, Video, and Audio (DVA+)**, and a brand-new **Full-Funnel Campaigns** format. If you manage your own PPC, this is the kind of structural change that quietly reshuffles what "effective campaign management" even means. Here's what's actually in it, and what to do with your campaigns starting now.

## What changed, specifically

Three things matter most for sellers, not agencies buying nine-figure DSP packages:

- **Full-Funnel Campaigns** tie Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, and streaming TV/video into a single campaign object. You hand Amazon's AI the products, creative assets, and a budget; it decides format mix and optimizes toward outcomes like new-to-brand customers and Amazon's "Long-Term Sales" (LTS) metric instead of just ACOS. It requires a **$100/day minimum budget** and is live for all US advertisers now.
- **DVA+** merges Sponsored Display, Sponsored TV, and programmatic display/video/audio buying into one campaign type. There's a simplified mode (pick an objective, budget, and creative, let Amazon handle targeting) and an advanced mode that keeps manual control over targeting, deal terms, supply sources, frequency, and bids.
- The **conversational Ads Agent chat** — a more capable version of the natural-language assistant Amazon introduced at last year's unBoxed — now handles media planning, AI targeting suggestions, campaign guidance, and analytics questions across 20 marketplaces. Amazon's own data claims advertisers who applied the tool's natural-language targeting recommendations saw 25%+ more unique customers while cutting cost-per-impression by over 10% on average.

The direction is unambiguous: Amazon wants fewer manual levers and more "tell it what you want, approve what it proposes." That's useful, but it also means the sellers who keep a human hand on strategy will outperform the ones who let the agent run unsupervised — the same pattern we saw when automated bidding first rolled out years ago.

## What to actually do about it

**1. Don't let Full-Funnel Campaigns touch your core ASINs yet.** A $100/day minimum and optimization toward new-to-brand and LTS metrics instead of ACOS is a meaningfully different mandate than what most sellers optimize for. Test it on a secondary, well-photographed ASIN with steady conversion history first, and track it against a control Sponsored Products campaign for at least three weeks before you decide it's additive rather than just eating budget your best keywords used to get.

**2. Treat the chat agent as a draft generator, not an approver.** The natural-language interface will propose targeting, budget shifts, and bid changes. Read every recommendation before you approve it, the same way you'd review a junior PPC analyst's first pass — especially on negative keyword suggestions and audience expansion, which is where "AI-recommended" targeting most often quietly drifts away from your actual buyer.

**3. Re-audit your dayparting and placement modifiers before migrating to DVA+.** Consolidating Sponsored Display, Sponsored TV, and programmatic buying into one campaign type is a chance for settings you set months ago (and forgot about) to get pulled forward into inventory you never evaluated — the same failure mode we flagged when Amazon auto-enrolled Sponsored Products into creator content back in August. If you've been running Sponsored Display with tight dayparting to avoid junk hours, verify that logic actually carries into DVA+'s simplified mode rather than assuming it does.

**4. Keep your keyword strategy manual where it's working.** Nothing here removes the value of a disciplined match-type structure — exact match on proven converters, phrase match for discovery, broad match feeding your search term reports. The Ads Agent chat is additive for finding gaps in your targeting, not a reason to abandon a keyword architecture that's already converting. Use it to surface terms you're missing, then vet and add them manually.

**5. Separate creative testing from campaign-type testing.** Full-Funnel and DVA+ both lean harder on creative than Sponsored Products ever did — video and display assets are now core inputs the AI optimizes around, not afterthoughts. If your product images and video haven't been refreshed to compete in a video/TV placement, upgrading your campaign structure won't fix an underlying creative gap. Fix the creative first, or the new campaign types will just optimize a weak asset faster.

## The bottom line

Amazon is betting that most advertisers want less manual control, not more. For a lot of sellers running lean teams, that's genuinely useful — fewer dashboards, less bid-tweaking busywork. But "the AI decides format mix" only works well when the inputs it's optimizing — your creative, your product data, your targeting logic — are already solid. Right now, the sellers who benefit fastest from Amazon Ads Agent are the ones who go in with a clean keyword structure and strong creative already in place, and use the new tools to scale what's working rather than to paper over what isn't.
